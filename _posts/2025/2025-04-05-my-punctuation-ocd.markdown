You may have landed here because I had just apologized for my obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) over punctuation in a GitHub comment and requested changes to your pull request, or you noticed me adding periods to a sentence in the otherwise excellent document you have authored. This is not the first time, and my condition manifests itself in some interesting ways. 

![]({{ site.url }}/images/posts/2025/2025-04-05-my-punctuation-ocd/shoes.gif)

Consider the following, seemingly well-formed sentence.

![]({{ site.url }}/images/posts/2025/2025-04-05-my-punctuation-ocd/words-ok.png)

This is what I actually see.

![]({{ site.url }}/images/posts/2025/2025-04-05-my-punctuation-ocd/words-ocd.png)

This must be, obviously, a _you_ problem! How can you fix it? Add a period to this complete sentence.

![]({{ site.url }}/images/posts/2025/2025-04-05-my-punctuation-ocd/words-fix.png)

Quite seriously, the gist of the issue is that I am simply not capable of reading text that is not properly punctuated. It's not you, it's me. Please add a period.

My rule of thumb for dealing with this condition is that _a complete sentence should end with a period_. Don't add a period to `a red brown fox jumped over a lazy dog` or `A Red Brown Fox Jumped Over a Lazy Dog`. 

Consistently punctuate list items, such as either all items are full, capitalized sentences, or none of them are. The following are good examples.

```
1. One red brown fox jumped over a lazy dog.
2. Two red brown foxes jumped over a lazy dog.
```

```
- one red brown fox jumped over a lazy dog
- two red brown foxes jumped over a lazy dog
```

After a very deep breath I may be able to tolerate a period at the end of a list, as long as the items are capitalized consistently, but this is not recommended. Periods are cheap.

```
1. One red brown fox jumped over a lazy dog
2. Two red brown foxes jumped over a lazy dog
3. Three red brown foxes jumped over a lazy dog.
```

There's "attention to detail" and there's "OCD". I am sorry for my OCD.

While you are here, you should also know that I am also a compulsive sorter. For example, I organize my personal documents by year, going back to 1994 and photos by date. 

![]({{ site.url }}/images/posts/2025/2025-04-05-my-punctuation-ocd/folders.gif)

There's more.

In 2016 I had to [invent a structure for my local git clones](/2016/03/25/a-directory-structure-for-oss-and-work-github-clones.html) after having an episode. I have [hundreds of labels to sort through GitHub notifications](/2022/08/08/managing-github-notifications-in-gmail.html). The posts in this blog are [similarly organized](https://github.com/dblock/code.dblock.org/tree/gh-pages/_posts), and the fact that there's both a `_posts` and a `posts` folder in Jekyll is currently very triggering. I am thrown off by lines [wrapping in markdown](/2021/06/07/to-wrap-or-not-to-wrap-in-markdown.html), and I wrote [a Danger plugin](https://github.com/dblock/danger-changelog) to ensure consistent sentence formatting in a project's CHANGELOG.md.

While writing this post, I also realized that I am not as triggered by lack of order or punctuation in books. I quite enjoy the chaos of William S. Burroughs' "Naked Lunch". I wonder what it is about computers that causes this in me?
